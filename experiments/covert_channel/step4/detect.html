The attacker hides in its average, not in its rhythm

What it means

The covert channel is detectable — but not by the obvious signal. A detector that flags heavy memory users misses it entirely: duty-cycling makes the attacker's average bandwidth (129 req/epoch) lower than steady lbm (176) or bwaves (211), so it reads as a light, innocent tenant.

And the average is the attacker's to tune — denser coding or higher duty slides it anywhere inside the benign band. The temporal structure is not: the on/off modulation is the channel itself. A burstiness detector separates the two perfectly (AUC 1.00).

So the defense is temporal

Mean-rate bandwidth accounting is the wrong tool. A monitor should watch the shape of a core's traffic — variance, idle gaps, periodicity — not its average.

This also bounds the attacker: to evade a structure-aware detector it must spread transmission so thinly that it loses the channel (see Step 2's rate limit). Detectability and capacity trade off directly.

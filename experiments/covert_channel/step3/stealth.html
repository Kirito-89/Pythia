How little effort does the channel need?

The sender transmits a fixed 1, but its duty — the fraction of time it actually floods memory — is swept from 100% down to 3%. At each level we ask how well the receiver still separates the signal, and how much bandwidth the sender had to spend to do it. A receiver that reads Pythia's own no-prefetch rate keeps a near-perfect channel down to 6% duty.

What that effort costs — sender's injected bandwidth

Attacker-core DRAM requests per megacycle during the transmission window. The footprint falls much more slowly than the duty: cutting effort buys only a little quiet.

What it means

The channel is cheap to run. An attacker need not flood continuously: driving memory just 6–10% of the time still delivers a clean channel.

A receiver that reads Pythia's learned policy (how often it chooses not to prefetch) beats one reading raw IPC by a wide margin at low effort — AUC 0.99 vs 0.91 at 6% duty, and 0.95 vs 0.80 at 3%. The prefetcher's adaptation is the more sensitive antenna.

The honest caveat

Low duty does not mean a small footprint. Injected bandwidth is sublinear in duty: at 60% duty the sender still emits 97% of full bandwidth, and even at the 6% knee it emits 56%.

So the stealth win is on the time-active axis, not yet the bandwidth axis. Whether that residual footprint hides among benign programs is exactly the Step 4 question.
